# Beyond the Listings

The people, local knowledge and practical questions behind Freedle & Associates

By Kim John Tuñacao | October 17, 2026

**Independent, unsolicited portfolio sample; not commissioned or endorsed by the company.**

A neighborhood is more than a pin on a map. A home search in the Denver metro can involve remarkably different commutes, ownership costs, building styles and daily routines, even when two listings are only a short drive apart. That complexity is where Freedle & Associates has chosen to make its case: not merely by showcasing homes, but by helping clients ask better questions before choosing one.

Based in Highlands Ranch, the boutique, family-run real estate team serves buyers, sellers and people relocating across Denver and the South Metro area. It also produces Southern Denver Living, a collection of neighborhood videos, relocation explainers and local-market content. Taken together, the firm's public work offers a revealing picture of how it wants clients to make decisions: with context, not just a list price. [1]

## A team built around different kinds of experience

Four professionals appear in the firm's public team directory: Jake Freedle and Megan Freedle, both managing partners; Scott Freedle, managing broker; and Sara Schlabach, associate broker. Jake and Megan identify themselves as a brother-and-sister team in their on-camera introductions, while Scott writes about the pleasure of working with his children. That family connection is part of the firm's story, but it is not the only thing that distinguishes the people behind the name. [2][3]

### Jake Freedle: negotiation meets neighborhood storytelling

Jake's company biography describes him as a Colorado native whose work spans relocations, first-time and move-up buyers, complex negotiations and acreage properties. It reports more than 300 completed transactions and over $100 million in sales. Those career numbers are the firm's own claims, rather than figures independently audited for this article. More revealing than the totals, however, is the combination of real estate and public-facing education: his neighborhood tours are designed to translate local details into decisions a relocating buyer can understand. [4]

### Megan Freedle: clarity for buyers navigating change

Megan's biography describes more than eight years in real estate and over 130 closings, again as company-reported figures. Her stated focus includes first-time buyers, relocations, complicated timelines and clients buying and selling in the same move. The biography also mentions a background in nutrition and physical therapy; it does not establish that she holds a clinical license, and none is implied here. Her public positioning stresses explaining the process rather than assuming buyers already know how it works. [5]

### Scott Freedle: senior brokerage perspective

Scott brings the longer professional horizon. His official profile states more than 25 years in real estate, 500-plus transactions and $200 million-plus in career sales, with a background in finance. It names acreage and lifestyle properties among his areas of focus. For buyers considering land, wells, septic systems or complicated property features, that kind of subject matter requires careful due diligence and, where needed, specialist advice. The profile presents Scott as a broker who values preparation and disciplined representation. [6]

### Sara Schlabach: housing and community as connected work

Sara is the firm's listed associate broker. Her publicly indexed company biography describes more than a decade in nonprofit work supporting housing for people experiencing homelessness. A separate professional biography from Honeywood Garden Design corroborates her community-program background and identifies her as a Master Gardener and Master Composter. These are professional details, not reasons to assume how any particular client transaction will go. They do help explain why community and the spaces people inhabit recur in her published work. [2][7]

## What Southern Denver Living adds to a typical home search

The clearest expression of the company's brand may be Southern Denver Living. Its video library does not consist solely of property tours. It includes neighborhood walkthroughs, market discussions and a candidly titled video about reasons not to move to Denver. In that discussion, Jake and Megan acknowledge the metro's drawbacks instead of claiming every aspect of Colorado life is effortless. That willingness to address tradeoffs is a useful starting point for readers comparing unfamiliar places. [8]

The companion blog is unusually specific. A September 2026 guide compares Parker, Castle Rock, Highlands Ranch, Lone Tree and Centennial through housing choices, transportation, amenities and practical ownership questions. An October article on buying in Parker from out of state draws a sharp line between what a remote tour can reveal and what inspections, disclosures and official records still need to establish. Rather than treating a video as proof that a house is sound, it encourages buyers to document what remains uncertain. [9][10]

That distinction matters. A polished home video can show layout, light and presentation; it cannot confirm every tax obligation, association rule, insurance concern or hidden condition. Useful real estate content helps a reader identify which facts to check at the property level, and which questions to bring to a qualified professional.

## The services behind the content

The team's website describes help with buying, selling and relocation, along with luxury and acreage situations. Its relocation materials encourage buyers to compare the experience of daily life: recurring trips, access to amenities, neighborhood layout and how a location works beyond the first weekend visit. For someone weighing a more walkable area against a larger lot, for example, the most helpful question may be less 'Which community is best?' and more 'What does Tuesday look like from this address?' [11]

On the selling side, the firm describes a process built around pricing evidence, property presentation and an intentional launch. Depending on the listing, its advertised marketing approach can include professional photography, cinematic video, floor plans, staging, digital distribution and neighborhood storytelling. These are services the company says it may use; they should not be read as a guarantee that every home receives the same package or result. [12]

For distinctive homes, the public content also examines land-use and operational details that glossy marketing sometimes overlooks. The firm's guides discuss how acreage can bring questions about water, access, outbuildings, easements and maintenance. Not every buyer needs the same expertise, which is precisely why a useful first consultation should start with the property type and the decisions that could materially affect ownership.

## What published clients say, and what reviews cannot prove

Public feedback provides a second perspective, though not a scientific measure of service quality. On Zillow, reviewers describe experiences involving long home searches, negotiation, responsiveness and support through inspection or transaction complications. One 2026 review discusses remaining with an agent through a multi-year search and several contracts before the eventual purchase. These are individual customers' accounts, not audited guarantees of how future transactions will turn out. [13]

The firm's own testimonials feature similar themes, including personal attention and a willingness to work through extended searches. Readers should keep the source distinction in mind: testimonials selected for a business website are curated by that business, whereas a public review platform supplies a different, still imperfect, view. More persuasive than any single five-star score is the recurring detail in the accounts: clients value explanations, patience and steady communication when the process gets difficult. [14]

## Local connection beyond a listing appointment

Jake also created HRtbeat: The Highlands Ranch Podcast, an interview program about local people, businesses and community life. Apple Podcasts lists 17 episodes and active years of 2024 through 2025; this article does not assume that new episodes are currently being released. The project is relevant because it shows a professional interest in describing the place itself, not only the homes available within it. [15]

Sara's separate involvement in community-based programming and garden stewardship offers another example of professional experience reaching beyond a conventional property listing. Those pursuits are not a substitute for brokerage credentials, but they add dimension to the people a prospective client may meet.

## Questions worth asking before you choose an agent

A researched company profile should help a buyer or seller make a decision, not merely admire a business. Before hiring any South Metro Denver agent, including Freedle & Associates, consider asking: How would you compare two neighborhoods for my actual commute and budget? Which ownership costs should I verify for this specific property? Who coordinates inspections and reviews unusual land or association issues? What is included in a proposed listing-marketing plan, and how will we measure its progress? How will updates be communicated when a transaction becomes complicated?

These are practical questions rather than promises about results. Real estate representation should be evaluated through the agent's current license status, a clear service agreement, property-specific due diligence and your own conversation about expectations.

## A more informed way to find your place

What makes Freedle & Associates interesting is not only the family connection or the published career milestones. It is the effort to connect brokerage services with neighborhood education, property-specific questions and local storytelling. Buyers and sellers can use that public material to arrive at an initial conversation better prepared.

For people considering a move to or within South Metro Denver, the next sensible step is to explore the firm's neighborhood guides and team profiles, then compare what they offer against your own priorities. A good real estate decision begins with information that can survive a closer look. [1][11]
